# Hebrew Reading Decoding (פענוח) for Ages 5-7 — Pedagogy Research Brief

**Feature this informs:** Short videos that TEACH Hebrew reading (decoding) to kids 5-7, one video per nikkud (vowel sign), with **sub-word (letter+nikkud / syllable) highlighting synced to the exact sound**. Progression: isolated letter+nikkud → CV syllable → blend across syllables → whole word.

**Bottom line:** The pedagogy strongly supports our design. Israeli first-grade reading IS synthetic phonics on pointed (nikkud) script: grapheme→phoneme mapping, then blending (מיזוג/צירוף) into words. The core unit is the **צירוף (CV cluster = consonant+vowel, e.g. בָּ = "ba")** — exactly the sub-word unit we want to highlight. Our per-nikkud, letter+nikkud-first approach matches both the Ministry of Education framework and common classroom practice.

## Key findings

### (a) Order of introducing the vowel signs
- **Start with קמץ + פתח (both /a/)** — the near-universal first pair. Rationale given by an Israeli teacher guide: "כיוון שהן נמצאות ברוב המילים בשפה העברית והן קלות להגייה" (they appear in most Hebrew words and are easy to pronounce). (ezed.co.il)
- Then **צירי + סגול (/e/)** → **חיריק (/i/)** → **חולם (/o/)** → **שורוק + קובוץ (/u/)**. This is the sequence presented as the teaching order on ezed.co.il and corroborated by graded worksheet sets (Or HaChaim) that group "קמץ פתח" first, then "קמץ פתח צירה סגול."
- **Vowels are taught as same-sound pairs** (תנועות גדולות/קטנות): קמץ/פתח = A, צירי/סגול = E, שורוק/קובוץ = U. Crucially, **שורוק and קובוץ are the SAME sound, differing only in spelling** — explicitly called out as a known confusion point even in the Ministry's assessment guide. For OUR videos this means: teach the SOUND with שורוק first (more common in kids' words), flag קובוץ as "same sound, different look."
- Reduced **חטף** vowels (חטף פתח/סגול/קמץ) come LAST. (ezed.co.il order: …dagesh → shin/sin dot → shva → chatafim.)

### (b) Synthetic phonics (blending) vs analytic/whole-word
- **Israeli grade-1 reading is explicitly synthetic.** Ministry of Education: first-graders read via "פענוח פונולוגי" — "ממירים יחידות גרפמיות… ביחידות פונולוגיות… **ומצרפים את היחידות האלה לכדי מילים**" (convert graphemes to phonemes and BLEND them into words). (Ministry of Education מדריך למורה)
- Classroom confirmation, in a teacher's own words: "**קריאה של כל צליל בנפרד, קריאה שוטפת של המילה שהתקבלה**" — read each sound separately, then read the resulting word smoothly. (mora-meyuchedet) This is precisely our progression: letter+nikkud → CV syllable → blend to word.
- The general-evidence base backs this: **systematic synthetic phonics** (letter-sounds first, then blend all-through-the-word) outperforms non-systematic approaches for word reading, especially for struggling readers (US National Reading Panel 2000; Clackmannanshire studies, Scotland). (Synthetic phonics, Wikipedia)

### (c) How שווא and דגש are introduced
- **דגש קל (B/K/P vs V/CH/F)** is taught because it changes the letter's sound; grade 1 focuses on **בּ/כּ/פּ** (the "אותיות רפות" distinction). Dagesh hazaq (consonant doubling) is a later/secondary idea. (ezed.co.il)
- **שווא** comes AFTER the full vowels, and is split: **שווא נח** (silent/closing, functions like a consonant, e.g. מִגְ-דַּל) is introduced first, then **שווא נע** (a "mini-vowel" sounding like /e/, e.g. מְלָפְפוֹן) — often taught via the conjunction וְ / לְ. (ezed.co.il)
- Practical signal from the Ministry assessment: the national decoding task EXCLUDES the fricative (רפויה) forms of ב/כ/פ from its syllable list — i.e., the ב/כ/פ dagesh distinction is treated as a deliberate, later-stage accuracy target, not a day-one item.

### (d) The Ministry framework & the core decoding unit
- Reading rests on the **alphabetic principle (עקרון אלפביתי)**: letters and nikkud signs map to consonants and vowels, and combine "כאבני הבניין של המילים" (as the building blocks of words). (Ministry of Education)
- **The core sub-word unit is the צירוף (CV cluster):** "המונח 'צירוף' נוגע ליחידה פונולוגית של עיצור ותנועה (למשל ba), המיוצגת בכתב כאות מנוקדת… (למשל בָּ)". A pointed letter = a CV syllable. **This is exactly what our videos should highlight.** (Ministry of Education, footnote 3)
- **Phonological-awareness ladder** (large → small): children first hear syllables, then the צירוף (onset cluster, e.g. "ba" in ברבור), then the single phoneme. Our video structure should mirror this: hear the syllable, then zoom to the letter+nikkud.
- **Morphological (שורש+משקל) awareness** grows alongside decoding and aids it, but is a grade-1+ layer, not a 5-7 decoding-video concern. (Ministry of Education)

## Concrete takeaways for OUR feature

1. **One nikkud per video, ordered by the /a/ → /e/ → /i/ → /o/ → /u/ sequence**, starting with קמץ+פתח together (teach as the "A sound" pair). This matches both the Ministry model and teacher practice.
2. **Structure each video as the synthetic ladder:** (1) hear the target syllable, (2) isolate the letter+nikkud sound (the צירוף, e.g. בָּ = "ba"), (3) highlight the exact letter+nikkud as its sound plays, (4) blend 2-3 syllables (בָּ + בָּ → בָּבָּ), (5) reveal the whole word. This is literally "read each sound, then read the word smoothly."
3. **Sub-word highlight target = the pointed letter (אות מנוקדת)**, not a finer split of the vowel mark alone — the Ministry defines the decodable unit as the consonant+vowel cluster. Highlight the whole בָּ glyph as "ba" is spoken.
4. **Teach שורוק first for /u/; treat קובוץ as "same sound, different costume"** — and say so explicitly, since even national materials flag the confusion.
5. **Keep דגש to the בּ/כּ/פּ (בג"ד כפ"ת) sound-change story for this age band** ("the dot makes it a hard sound"); save דגש חזק and all חטף forms for later videos.
6. **שווא gets its own video, late in the series**, framed as "the quiet/resting dot" (שווא נח) before the "whisper" שווא נע — and only after the five full vowels are secure.
7. **Pace: a few sounds per week, one well before the next** (synthetic-phonics pacing ~3-6 letter-sounds/week). Don't bundle multiple nikkud into one video.
8. **Anchor each nikkud to 2-3 real, frequent, imageable words** (the reason /a/ is taught first is frequency + ease) — pick the highest-frequency kid words that use that vowel.
