# 03 — Platform Rules, Monetization & Legal Risk (YouTube, 2025–2026)

## (a) "Made for Kids" (MFK) — rules & tradeoffs

**Legal basis.** MFK is how YouTube complies with COPPA (post the 2019 $170M Google/FTC settlement).
Creators are **legally required** to mark kids' content regardless of where they live. A "13+"
disclaimer does NOT exempt you — the FTC weighs other COPPA factors (child-appealing characters,
songs, activities). A whole channel can be designated MFK, not just per-video.

**Disabled features on MFK content (official list):**
- Comments · Notification bell · Cards and end screens · Video watermarks · Channel Memberships
- Donate button · Likes/dislikes on YouTube Music · Live chat + donations · Merch/ticketing shelf
- **Personalized advertising** (the big one) · Miniplayer playback · Super Chat/Stickers
- Save to playlist / Watch later · Autoplay on Home

**Channel-level removals:** Memberships, Notification bell, Posts (community tab), Ask Studio.

**Ads on MFK:** personalized ads, remarketing, and all third-party trackers **prohibited**. Only
**contextual ads** run. Prohibited ad categories on MFK include food & beverage (banned "regardless
of nutrition content"), dating, beauty/weight-loss, political, religious, violent, adult. YouTube
concedes MFK **"may result in a decrease in revenue."**

**Distribution nuance:** MFK videos are "more likely to be recommended alongside other children's
videos," but MFK status does NOT auto-include you in the YouTube Kids app — separate filter + human
review gated on the quality principles.

**Bottom line:** MFK strips nearly every community/retention/growth tool AND the highest-paying ad
format. It's a compliance floor, not a growth strategy.

## (b) Quality policy — what YouTube rewards vs. punishes

Developed with child-development specialists; applies to long-form AND Shorts; drives monetization
and YouTube Kids app inclusion.

**Rewarded ("high quality"):**
1. Looking after yourself & others — kindness, friendship, healthy habits
2. Learning & inspiring curiosity — school subjects, critical thinking
3. Creativity, play, imagination — storytelling, sing-alongs, crafts, original games
4. Life skills — **"a complete narrative (character development, plot, resolution)"** with a clear lesson
5. Shows them the world — diverse, relatable characters

**Punished (demonetized / suppressed):**
- Heavily promotional (unboxing, product focus)
- Encouraging negative behaviors (dangerous pranks, bullying, dishonesty)
- Deceptively educational (promises to teach colors/numbers, shows wrong info)
- **Hard to follow** — "confusing or make no sense," jumbled storylines, "often the result of mass
  production or autogeneration"
- Sensational/misleading, keyword stuffing
- **"Strange use of children's characters"** — known characters in inappropriate/risky scenarios
  (the Elsagate clause — directly implicates using famous IP)

**Enforcement:** high-quality MFK "gets raised up in recommendations"; a channel with a "strong
focus on low-quality MFK content" can be **suspended from the YPP**; individual videos get limited/no ads.

**2025 update — "inauthentic content" (15 July 2025).** The old "repetitious content" policy renamed,
explicitly covering "repetitive or mass-produced" work. Channels "where content feels interchangeable
from video to video are not allowed to monetise." Named violations: templated storylines, image
slideshows with no narrative, and **"AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates,
giving the impression of mass production."** Aimed squarely at the low-effort AI kids-content flood.
Circumvention (new channels to dodge suspension) "could lead to the termination of all channels."

**Implication for this factory:** each episode needs a REAL narrative arc (character development,
plot, resolution) — not a template swap. This aligns with the quality principles AND is the
anti-"AI slop" market positioning.

## (c) IP red lines — Stitch / Disney

**Confirmed: you cannot build a kids' channel on Disney's Stitch/Lilo, Marvel, or any Disney character.**

1. **Copyright strikes:** a valid removal request = a strike + content removed. **3 strikes =
   account + all associated channels terminated, can't create new YouTube channels.** Deleting the
   video doesn't remove a strike.
2. **Content ID:** Disney is among the most aggressive holders. A claim typically isn't a strike, but
   Disney can take your revenue, block the video, or escalate to removal (→ strike) if you dispute
   without basis. "Fan art" of a trademarked character is not fair use as a matter of law.
3. **Disney's own posture:** 1,250+ YouTube channels; the FTC just fined Disney **$10M (Sept 2025)**
   for mislabeling its own child-directed videos — proving both how seriously Disney treats its YouTube
   IP operation and how aggressively the space is policed. Disney extends enforcement even to fan works.

**Plus:** putting a "known character kids love" into your own scenario is itself a quality-policy
violation ("strange use of children's characters"), inviting demonetization on top of copyright.

**COPPA lesson from Disney's settlement:** Disney marked kids' videos "Not Made for Kids" at the
channel level, so 300+ child-directed videos ran targeted ads without parental consent. The FTC now
requires a per-video Audience Designation Program. **Mislabeling kids content is itself an FTC
enforcement target** — not just a settings toggle.

## (d) Platform verdict — YouTube-only, in practice

- **TikTok:** minimum age 13 (14 in Florida). Under-13s pushed into an "Under 13 Experience" — can
  watch and capture to their own device, but videos are not collected or viewable by other users; no
  public posting, profile, or messaging. **No brand/publishing path for under-13 content.**
- **Instagram (Meta):** also 13+; no under-13 content ecosystem.
- **YouTube:** the only major platform with a formal, legal, monetizable MFK framework + the dedicated
  YouTube Kids app.

A kids' short-video strategy is effectively a **YouTube strategy** (main app MFK + YouTube Kids app
inclusion as the prize).

## (e) Monetization expectations

- **Shorts ad RPM is already low:** creators report **~3¢–7¢ RPM** ($30–$70 per million views); some
  tables put the floor at 1¢. (Long-form by contrast: $1–$30 RPM.)
- **MFK Shorts sit at the bottom of even that range** — personalized ads banned, only lower-value
  contextual ads run.
- **YPP thresholds:** 1,000 subs + 10M public Shorts views/90 days (or 4,000 long-form watch-hours/yr)
  for ad revenue sharing; creators keep **45%** of allocated Shorts ad revenue. A lower tier (500 subs
  + 3M Shorts views) unlocks fan-funding only — but Super Thanks/Chat/Stickers and Memberships are all
  disabled on MFK content anyway, so the fan-funding tier is largely useless for kids' channels.

**Realistic conclusion:** ad revenue alone on MFK Shorts is near-negligible at small scale. The actual
kids' business model is the **Cocomelon/Moonbug playbook** — YouTube for reach, monetize off-platform:
licensing (Moonbug on Netflix/Hulu), music/songs on **Spotify Kids**, and merch/toys (Cocomelon's 2.5B
monthly views converted into toys and albums). **YouTube Shorts is top-of-funnel; the money is in the
owned IP, not the ads.**
