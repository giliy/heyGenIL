# 02 — The Hebrew-Language Kids' Content Landscape (Israel, ages 2–8)

Method note: subscriber figures from the Hebrew Wikipedia most-subscribed-Israeli-channels ranking
(data "as of June 2025," citing Socialblade) and dated Israeli press — live YouTube stats pages are
JS shells that wouldn't render through the fetch tool.

## (a) Channel landscape — four tiers

**Key structural fact: the biggest "Israeli" kids channels are not Hebrew-language products.**

### Tier 1 — Giants (global/export plays that happen to be Israeli-founded)
| Channel | Subs (Jun 2025) | Language | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TuTiTu** (טוטיטו) | **5.52M** (#1 Israeli channel of ANY kind) | English / near-wordless | 3D "toy comes to life" object-assembly, ages 1–3; deliberately language-light, exports to 16 languages |
| **Masha and the Bear — Hebrew** (מאשה והדוב) | **3.06M** | Hebrew **dub** | Russian import, Hebrew-dubbed — the biggest "Hebrew" kids channel is not original |
| Disney Channel Israel | 1.25M | Hebrew dub | Imported US content |

### Tier 2 — Israeli-origin baby brands (the "Israeli baby-content diaspora")
Israel punches far above its weight as a *creator* of global baby content:
- **BabyTV** (בייבי טיווי) — founded in Israel 2003; now Disney-owned internationally, Talit-owned
  within Israel. Hebrew channel ≈ **903K subs (Jun 2025)**, up from ~400K in 2020. Gentle in-house
  shows across nine developmental themes, ad-free, ages 0–4.
- **BabyFirst** — Israeli-American-founded (Guy Oranim & Sharon Rechter), US-domiciled; Hebrew is
  one of its broadcast languages. (Two separate, competing networks — both trace to Israel.)

### Tier 3 — The Hebrew-TV establishment on YouTube
| Channel | Subs (Jun 2025) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **כאן חינוכית — Kan Kids / Kan Hinuchit** | **1.00M** (Gold Play Button April 2026) | Public broadcaster's kids arm. Prestige Hebrew-original home: פרפר נחמד, בבית של פיסטוק, רגע עם דודלי, שקשוקה, + dubs of Bluey, Dinosaur Train |
| **Hop! Israeli Childhood** (הופ! ילדות ישראלית) | 806K | Hop!'s music/classics arm — the nostalgia + שירי ילדים catalog |
| **ערוץ הופ! — Hop! Channel** | 677K | Main preschool TV channel (founded 2000, ages 0–7); home of the Israeli רחוב סומסום (Rechov Sumsum, local Sesame Street) |
| HOT VOD Young | 605K | Cable VOD arm |

### Tier 4 — Beloved human characters (huge culturally, mid-tier on YouTube subs)
- **יובל המבולבל (Yuval HaMebulbal / Yuval Shem-Tov)** — the dominant costumed live clown of the
  2000s–2010s (Hop!, Festigal, DVDs). Songs, sketches, energetic stage shows, sidekick מר עגבנייה
  (Mr. Tomato). **Does NOT appear in the top-subscriber ranking** — reach built on TV/DVD/stage;
  YouTube under-indexes his fame (individual film uploads have crossed 11.5M views).
- **מיכל הקטנה (Michal HaKtana / Michal Weitzman)** — live performer called "מלכת ילדים" (children's
  queen). Songs, stage musicals, books; shows on yes, Hop!, Yoyo.
- **קופיקו (Kofiko)** — the classic cheeky monkey from Tamar Bornstein-Lazar's books (since 1951,
  longest-running Hebrew kids' book series), adapted to 1993 TV, now on YouTube. Cross-generational IP.
- **The Festigal (פסטיגל)** — the annual Hanukkah children's musical (since 1981) — the cultural
  kingmaker that *launches* these stars.

### Smaller Hebrew-native originals (the realistic peer set for a new channel)
- **טופקידס (TopKids)** — Hebrew educational: shapes, colors, letters, numbers, transport, nature.
- **צופי (Tzofi)** — Hebrew toddler education: numbers, colors, animals, art, music.
- **EZtoddler** — teaches Hebrew letters (אותיות) + nikud + first English words, mascot איזי העיפרון (Izzy the Pencil).
- **משחקי הכיף (Mischakei HaKef)** — animals, space, holidays, letters, numbers + crafts.
- **הילימוד (Hilimud)** — host-led Hebrew alphabet, holidays, seasons, general knowledge.
- **לולי (Luli)** — Hop!'s sister baby channel (up to age 2).
- **ילדות ישראלית (Israeli Childhood)** — the Hebrew kids-song/classics catalog brand.

### Short-form specifically
YouTube Kids launched in Israel **Oct 20, 2020**, with local content incl. Kan Educational, Yuval
HaMebulbal, Nick Jr., WIZZ. In **April 2026 YouTube rolled out a control letting parents cap kids'
Shorts minutes — down to zero** — and Israeli parent influencers (e.g., אמאלה אני אבא) actively
recommend blocking Shorts/YouTube Kids in favor of Netflix/Disney+. **Direct evidence that Israeli
toddlers ARE watching Shorts at scale** — enough that parents and YouTube built tools to restrict it.

## (b) The Hebrew kids' song canon + copyright status

Israeli copyright = **life + 70 years**. The canon falls into three generations (Hebrew Wikipedia's
שירי ילדים category lists ~171 song articles).

### Generation 1 — Pre-state / early-state (before ~1954) → mostly PUBLIC DOMAIN
Safe, evergreen, "every Israeli child knows them" bedrock:
- **גשם גשם מטפטף** — "Rain, Rain, Pitter-Patter"
- **השקדיה פורחת** — "The Almond Tree Is Blooming" (Tu Bishvat)
- **באנו חושך לגרש** — "We Came to Expel the Darkness" (Hanukkah)
- **עשר אצבעות לי יש** — "I Have Ten Fingers"
- **סלינו על כתפינו** — "Our Baskets on Our Shoulders" (Shavuot)
- **לדוד משה הייתה חווה** — "Uncle Moshe Had a Farm" (Hebrew Old MacDonald)
- **אן דן דינו** — counting rhyme (folk)
- **נדנדה** — "Seesaw"; **נסדר מעגל גדול** — "Let's Form a Big Circle" (play songs, largely folk)
- ⚠️ **The Levin Kipnis (לוין קיפניס) catalog is NOT public domain** — he died 1990, copyrighted until ~2060.

### Generation 2 — Educational-TV golden age (1970s–1980s) → COPYRIGHTED
The heart of what today's parents grew up on. **NOT free to use:**
- **תמיד נשאר אני** — "I Always Remain Me" — written by **דתיה בן דור (Datya Ben Dor, b. 1944, living)**
  in 1983, first performed by Uzi Hitman on פרפר נחמד. Firmly under copyright for decades.
- The wider **עוזי חיטמן (Uzi Hitman)** and **מתי כספי (Mati Caspi)** children's repertoire — all copyrighted.
- **האוטו שלנו גדול וירוק**, **אליעזר והגזר**, **יונתן הקטן**, **מיכאל** — mostly still in copyright.

### Generation 3 — Character-star era (1990s–present) → COPYRIGHTED
All Yuval HaMebulbal, Michal HaKtana, Hop!, and Kan originals.

### Practical copyright guidance for a NEW channel
- **Safe to adapt freely:** genuinely pre-1954 folk songs and anonymous street/counting rhymes.
- **Do NOT assume the famous 1970s–80s TV songs are free** — licensing or original composition required.
- **Cleanest path: original songs in the *style* of the canon** — exactly what Hop!/Kan/the character
  stars all do.

## (c) Gap analysis — what a NEW Hebrew kids' shorts series could own

1. **The top of the market is "Israeli" but not "Hebrew."** No dominant Hebrew-first, Hebrew-speaking
   original at the top. A Hebrew-native channel serves the audience TuTiTu deliberately ignores.
2. **Educational Hebrew short-form is fragmented and low-budget.** The dedicated educators (TopKids,
   Tzofi, EZtoddler, Hilimud) are enthusiast-grade. **A professionally-produced Hebrew אותיות/מספרים/צבעים
   shorts series would immediately be best-in-class** — the imports can't teach the Hebrew alphabet,
   the local incumbents can't match premium craft. **The single clearest gap.**
3. **The beloved characters under-index on YouTube.** A native-digital Hebrew character designed for
   Shorts (rather than ported from TV) is unclaimed territory.
4. **Parents want Hebrew content but fear Shorts' downsides.** The winning positioning is **"the Shorts
   you don't have to feel guilty about":** Hebrew, educational, calm-paced (anti-Cocomelon overstimulation),
   ad-safe — explicitly marketed to the parents currently blocking YouTube Kids entirely.
5. **Cultural specificity is a moat.** Global dubs can't do Shabbat songs, chagim, letter-of-the-week,
   or Israeli playground rhymes authentically. שירי שבת, שירי חגים, and the public-domain Gen-1 canon
   give a new channel a content well the imports structurally cannot fill.

**Bottom line:** A premium, calm, **Hebrew-native** shorts series teaching אותיות/מספרים/צבעים +
original canon-style songs + chagim/Shabbat cultural content would own a lane that (a) the export
giants ignore, (b) the dub channels can't touch, and (c) the existing small Hebrew educators can't
match on production quality — while directly answering documented parental demand for trustworthy
Hebrew screen content.
